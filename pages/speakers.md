      <p class="speaker-talk"><span>Talk</span> Algorithms for the Anthropocene: The Promise and Pitfalls of Machine Learning in a Changing Arctic</p>
      <details class="abstract speaker-abstract">
        <summary>Abstract</summary>
        <blockquote>
          <p>The Arctic is warming faster than any other region on Earth, raising the risk that carbon long stored in frozen soils will be released to the atmosphere and further intensify climate warming. The scale and inaccessibility of the Arctic mean remote characterization is the rule, rather than the exception, and recent advancements in satellite imaging, computing infrastructure, and machine learning promise to harness a "big data" approach to mapping permafrost landscape change. But what data and knowledge are needed to move from just seeing change to understanding its causes and consequences? In this talk, I will present our recent progress in studying changing permafrost landscapes, highlighting the complementary strengths of building and testing conceptual models alongside computational analysis.</p>
        </blockquote>
      </details>
      <p class="speaker-bio">Joanmarie Del Vecchio is a geomorphologist who uses field observations and remotely sensed data from satellites and drones to study how landscapes respond to a changing climate. Her work spans permafrost landscapes, surface processes, and computational and "big data" approaches to Earth science, including models for identifying Arctic landforms and studies of how permafrost thaw shapes rivers, erosion, and vegetation.</p>

      <p class="speaker-talk"><span>Talk</span> Modeling Urban Climates: Challenges and Opportunities with AI</p>
      <details class="abstract speaker-abstract">
        <summary>Abstract</summary>
        <blockquote>
          <p>Modeling the climate of cities is a challenging problem. Built environments have complex, fine-scale heterogeneous 3D layouts and unique material properties that differ substantially from natural landscapes. This complexity introduces a challenge for city-scale planning: understanding the effect of urban patterns on local weather is a non-trivial task requiring detailed building data in addition to larger-scale climate patterns.</p>
          <p>In this talk I will introduce the physics of urban climate and offer a brief introduction to traditional modeling paradigms. I will then showcase recent work from our group on addressing these challenges using AI techniques tailored to urban-scale climate modeling aimed at supporting urban planning. I will introduce uMORPH, a global urban canopy dataset, and early applications in downscaling climate fields to neighborhood scales.</p>
        </blockquote>
      </details>
      <p class="speaker-bio">Luis Ortiz is co-Director of the Virginia Climate Center at George Mason and Acting State Climatologist for Virginia. He studies how cities interact with the atmosphere in a changing climate, and how those interactions affect people and infrastructure. His work uses high-resolution dynamical and statistical modeling of urban weather and climate to analyze impacts ranging from energy demand to exposure to weather extremes.</p>

      <p class="speaker-talk"><span>Talk</span> Some Lessons from Smart Transportation and Beyond to Help Plan for a Better Planet</p>
      <details class="abstract speaker-abstract">
        <summary>Abstract</summary>
        <blockquote>
          <p>In this invited talk, which is an extension of an accepted position paper, we argue that for using planning techniques for a better planet, one can benefit from lessons learnt from previous efforts in using computational and Artificial Intelligence (AI) methods for sustainability problems. We begin by focusing on transportation and provide a brief overview of promising initiatives in the past in developing and developed countries around the world, and highlight their key ideas. We then use this experience to draw lessons on the socio-technical conditions when AI-based methods may work for climate change (and when not). Since sustainability exemplifies a system of system domain where no system (dimension – like transportation) can work in isolation from others, we will also discuss how the lessons could generalize to other systems (dimensions) where we have worked including water, energy and governance. The lessons emphasize the need to engage stakeholders, grow open data and skill ecosystem, and communicate with sensitivity to sponsors. We argue that AI planning provides a powerful tool for addressing the challenges of the planet, like climate change, but success needs careful practical considerations that were not emphasized in the past.</p>
        </blockquote>
      </details>
      <p class="speaker-bio">Biplav Srivastava is a Professor of Computer Science at the AI Institute and Department of Computer Science at the University of South Carolina (USC) which he joined in 2020 after two decades in industrial research. He has worked on tackling research problems in sustainability using AI and planning methods from around the world since 2010, leading to many publications, tutorials, and prototypes. <em>Trustworthy AI, aligned to human values and sustainable development, is the need of the hour.</em> He directs the <a href="https://ai4society.github.io/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">AI for Society</a> group at USC that is working on promoting goal-oriented, ethical, human-machine collaboration using learning and reasoning.</p>
